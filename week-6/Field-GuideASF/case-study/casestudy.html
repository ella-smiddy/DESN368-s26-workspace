Case Study

Design Decisions

Colors: I wanted the colors to feel drawn striaght from the board game. I had the idea for it to come off as playful and bright, but also simple and easy to read. When creating the design in Figma, I had a main image of the board game box and the board itself. I then used the eyedrop tool on to extract the exact colors I wanted to make it feel fully connected to the real game.

Layout: I decided to just do a basic layout with the navbar to the left, stuck. I did my best to create a sensible flow of direction. Meaning, I wanted it to feel just like reading the intructions from the real game. It had to start with the basic contents in the box, then into what each cards means, along to the actual gameplay.

Technical Challenges

I was really struggling with my banner image because I kept accidently cutting it off or stretching it too far, so I am not super haooy with the outcome of that, but it works for now. Some of the smaller images took me more time to get just right as well. My laptop was NOT wanting to cooroperate and show the dimensions in my figma reference site. So I landed on the good old guess and check method. I do like how they turned out.

Another struggle I had was linking my nav bar and keeping the styles, it was just small mistakes here and there that kept confusing the computer, but eventually with outside help I was able to keep my stles and have my buttons actually work! This then made it easier to fix my links in the footer later on.
